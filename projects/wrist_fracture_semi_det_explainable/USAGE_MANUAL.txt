================================================================================
PAEDIATRIC WRIST-FRACTURE DETECTOR WITH BUILT-IN FRACTURE VISUALISATION
Research artefact - not a clinical decision-making device.  See section 5.
================================================================================

1. WHAT IT DOES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Input : one paediatric wrist radiograph (frontal or lateral view), 16-bit or
        8-bit PNG, any size.  One film at a time; views are read independently.
Output: (a) a fracture probability between 0 and 1
        (b) an "evidence map" painted over the film showing where the finding is
        (c) a box drawn around the fractured region, with its own confidence
        (d) a machine-readable record of all of the above

The point of the design is that (a) and (b) cannot disagree.  The model does not
make a fracture/no-fracture judgement and then get asked to justify it.  It
produces only the evidence map, and the overall probability is a fixed
mathematical read-out of that map - essentially "how strong is the single
strongest spot".  So the probability IS the highlighted region.  If the model
calls a film fractured, something on that film is lit up, and that is what you
are shown.  The box is read off the same map.

2. HOW TO RUN IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
From the repository root:

  # new radiographs: put PNG files in one folder
  uv run projects/20260910_09_wrist_fracture_det/predict.py --images /path/to/folder

  # reproduce the sealed test-set evaluation reported below
  uv run projects/20260910_09_wrist_fracture_det/predict.py --test

For --images, results are printed per film and written to
  /path/to/folder/predictions/report.json
containing, for each film, the fracture probability and the marked region(s) as
[x1, y1, x2, y2] in the ORIGINAL pixel coordinates of that film.

For --test, outputs land in projects/20260910_09_wrist_fracture_det/ :
  test_metrics.json      overall performance figures
  test_predictions.csv   one row per film (see section 4)
  test_report.json       per-film probability, marked regions, radiologist boxes
  example_overlays/      24 sample pictures
and all 3,932 full-size overlay pictures (2.8 GB) are written to
  /path/to/your/external_data/gpwd_sep10_test_overlays/
In an overlay, RED is the model's marked region and GREEN is the radiologist's.

Model file: projects/20260910_09_wrist_fracture_det/run12/checkpoints/best.pth
Operating threshold: 0.0614 (probability at or above this = "fracture").
This threshold was chosen on the validation children only, never on the test set.

3. WHAT IT WAS TRAINED ON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GRAZPEDWRI-DX paediatric wrist radiographs, 6,091 children, ages 0.2-19 years.
Children were split so that no child appears in more than one group:
  training    3,898 children / 13,152 films
  validation    974 children /  3,243 films  (used for all tuning decisions)
  sealed test 1,219 children /  3,932 films  (opened once, at the very end)

Only about 820 training films carried a radiologist-drawn outline of the
fracture; the rest carried only a yes/no note.  The model learns precise
localisation from the outlined films, learns "nothing anywhere" from every
fracture-free film, and learns from the yes/no films through a rule that says
"if this film is fractured, some location must light up" without being told
which.  It is also taught to recognise four classes of fracture look-alike
(laterality letter markers, metalwork/casts, periosteal reaction, and other
bone/soft-tissue findings) so that it does not confuse them with fractures.

4. PERFORMANCE ON THE SEALED TEST SET (3,932 films, 1,219 children)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Measured once, after all development was finished.

Primary measure - detection AND correct placement of the mark
  fracture AP@0.5 (overlap >= 50% with the true fracture)      0.839
  (validation value was 0.878; see the caveat in section 5)

Telling fractured from non-fractured films
  discrimination (AUC; 1.0 perfect, 0.5 coin-flip)             0.980
  at the operating threshold 0.0614:
    sensitivity (fractures correctly flagged)                  0.951   (2384/2508)
    specificity (normal films correctly cleared)               0.924   (1316/1424)
    positive predictive value                                  0.957
    negative predictive value                                  0.914
    missed fractures                                           124
    false alarms                                               108

Does it point at the right place?
  hottest point falls inside the true fracture                 0.963
  among flagged fractured films, mark overlaps the fracture    0.958
  median overlap of the mark with the true fracture            0.748
  detection sensitivity at 1 false mark per film               0.882
  fracture-free films with no mark at all                      0.699
  average marks per film: 0.39 (normal)   1.63 (fractured)

By age
  0-5 years    (n=259)   sensitivity 0.969   specificity 0.971
  5-9 years    (n=883)   sensitivity 0.973   specificity 0.893
  9-13 years  (n=1631)   sensitivity 0.946   specificity 0.946
  13-20 years (n=1159)   sensitivity 0.923   specificity 0.906

By view
  frontal (n=1942)       sensitivity 0.944   specificity 0.919
  lateral (n=1974)       sensitivity 0.956   specificity 0.929

Missed fractures were usually missed with conviction: the median probability on
the 124 missed films was 0.025, i.e. they did not sit just below the threshold.

5. LIMITATIONS AND INTENDED USE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* This is a research artefact. It is not a medical device, has no regulatory
  clearance, and must not be used to make or withhold clinical decisions.
* Single site, single dataset (Graz). Performance on other hospitals, other
  X-ray equipment or other populations is unknown and would need separate
  testing.
* WRIST ONLY. It has never been shown any other body part. Given a film of
  another region it will still output a number, and that number is meaningless.
* Per-film, not per-patient. Frontal and lateral views are read independently
  and are not combined, so the two views of one wrist may disagree.
* Fracture presence only. It does not classify fracture type, AO grade,
  displacement, angulation, age of the injury, or healing status.
* The reference standard is one radiologist's reading recorded in the dataset,
  not surgical or follow-up confirmation, and apparent false alarms are not
  always errors.
* The primary localisation figure is uncertain. It rests on a validation subset
  containing only 139 outlined fractures; retraining the identical
  configuration with a different random seed moved it from 0.878 to 0.830. The
  sealed-test value of 0.839 sits inside that band. The film-level figures
  (sensitivity, specificity, discrimination) are far more stable - they are
  computed on thousands of films and varied by less than 0.01 between seeds.
* The operating point favours sensitivity, as agreed for a missed-fracture-
  averse setting. It produces roughly one false alarm per 13 normal films.
* Accepting a mark as correct required 50% overlap with the radiologist's box.
  A mark that lands on the fracture but is drawn larger or smaller counts as
  wrong under that rule even though it would be clinically useful.
